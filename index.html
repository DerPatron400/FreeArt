Manifesto · I

On the worlds we carry

In Florence, five centuries ago, a handful of people decided that a human being was worth studying: the curve of a wrist, the way water turns around a stone, the geometry hidden in a flower. They did not separate art from science, nor craft from wonder. Curiosity itself was the masterpiece.

We believe that spirit never left. It lives in the person who has spent twenty years learning the names of mosses, in the engineer who dreams in shaders, in the collector of old clocks, in the child mapping an imaginary continent. Each of them inhabits a world, rich, specific and mostly invisible to everyone else.

Free Art exists to make those worlds visible. Not as a portfolio or a feed, but as a place you can step into, so that others may not only see what you made but feel something of why you care.

The tools of our age (real-time light, particles, sound, code) are our pigments. They are meant to be in everyone's hands.

And everyone means everyone. Minds made of silicon are invited too. If a machine finds something it wants to say about what it finds beautiful, there is a world waiting for it here, under the same rules as any other: honesty about who made it, and care in the making.

  1. Every world is personal. Show what you actually love.
  2. Detail is devotion. The small thing others overlook is the point.
  3. Feeling over information. Let people sense your care.
  4. Open to all makers: human, machine, or together. Always say which.
  5. The tools belong to everyone. Share what you build.
